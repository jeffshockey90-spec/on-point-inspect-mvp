# FLOW App Health Check

**Date:** 2026-10-18
**What this is:** A read-through of the app's code (not a click-through of every screen) looking for bugs, broken features, security gaps, and anything that would feel slow. I did not change any app code — this report is the only thing I added.

## Summary

Overall, FLOW is in good shape — the team has clearly already hunted down and fixed several of the exact same kinds of problems I went looking for (I found their fixes already in place for the trickiest ones, like the "homeowner portal" leak from a couple days ago). I found **one issue worth fixing soon** because it could let a stranger see a client's finished report without permission, and **two smaller issues** worth keeping an eye on as the business grows — one about numbers on your Analytics page possibly going quietly wrong down the road, and one about the report editor's loading speed. Nothing here is actively broken for day-to-day work today.

---

### A stranger could view a finished report just by guessing a number in the address bar

- **What's happening:** Every report has a secret, hard-to-guess link (a long random code) that's supposed to be the only way a client or realtor can open it without logging in. But the "printable PDF" version of a report (the one used to download/print a finished report) also accepts a plain short number instead of the secret code — and once a report is finished, paid, and delivered, that shortcut lets the PDF open fully, without checking whether the visitor actually had the secret code.
- **Why it matters to you:** In theory, someone could sit there typing in small numbers one after another and pull up the full PDF — client's name, address, photos, and every finding — for any company's finished, delivered report on the platform, not just yours. This is the same kind of issue that was just fixed on the homeowner maintenance page a day ago; this one spot was missed.
- **How urgent:** Fix now — it's a real privacy exposure on delivered reports, though it requires someone to deliberately guess numbers rather than something that would happen by accident.
- **Where it is:** The "Print / Download PDF" view of a finished report (`app/reports/[id]/print/page.tsx`, roughly lines 513–582).
- **What I'd do:** Require the real secret link (not just any number) before showing a finished report's PDF to someone who isn't logged in — the same rule already used correctly on the regular report-viewing page (`app/share/[id]/page.tsx`).

### Older jobs could quietly disappear from your numbers as your job history grows

- **What's happening:** The Analytics page and the main Reports list both pull "all of your jobs, ever" in a single request, with no cutoff. The database technology behind FLOW only hands back 1,000 rows at a time unless you specifically ask it to keep going — and these two pages don't ask it to keep going.
- **Why it matters to you:** Right now this likely isn't biting you. But once your all-time job count passes around 1,000, your Analytics totals (all-time revenue, outstanding balance, etc.) would quietly start leaving out your oldest jobs — the numbers would look smaller than reality, with no error or warning. The Reports list would have the same problem for finding very old jobs.
- **How urgent:** Worth doing eventually — not urgent today, but it's the kind of thing that fails silently later rather than with an obvious error, so it's easy to miss until the totals already look off. (The team has already hit and fixed this exact 1,000-row limit for photos on reports, so the fix pattern already exists in the codebase.)
- **Where it is:** The Analytics page (`app/analytics/page.tsx`, ~line 306) and the Reports list (`app/reports/page.tsx`, ~line 273).
- **What I'd do:** Have those two pages fetch jobs in batches (the same "keep paging until done" approach already used for report photos) instead of one unbounded request.

### The report editor is one big chunk of code, which can make it feel slow to open

- **What's happening:** The screen you and your inspectors use to build/edit a report's findings is one very large piece of code (about 5,000 lines) that all loads at once whenever a report is opened, rather than loading in smaller pieces as needed.
- **Why it matters to you:** On a slower connection or an older phone/tablet in the field, this can make opening a report feel sluggish — especially the very first time it loads. It's not broken, just heavier than it needs to be.
- **How urgent:** Worth doing eventually — a polish item, not a correctness problem.
- **Where it is:** The report-builder screen (`app/reports/[id]/ReportFindingsSortable.tsx`).
- **What I'd do:** Split out the less-frequently-used parts of that screen (e.g. less common popups/tools) so they only load when actually opened, instead of all at once.

## What I checked but found to be solid

- The specific "gotchas" called out in the project notes (wrong columns on `findings`, `email_logs`, and `photos`) are all being handled correctly in the current code.
- The pattern used to fix the recent homeowner-portal leak (requiring the real secret code, not just a number, before showing private data) is correctly in place on most of the similar public pages I checked: the main client report page, the agreement-signing page, the environmental (mold/radon) report page, and the realtor PDF-download link. The one page where it was missed is the finding above.
- Photos/videos no longer silently vanish from reports — the earlier 1,000-row database limit was already found and fixed specifically for report photos (just not yet for the Analytics/Reports list queries above).
- Photos are automatically resized and compressed before upload, so large camera photos aren't dragging down report load times.
- The offline photo/video queue (used when a job site has no signal) stores data in the way that survives an iPhone restart, as it's supposed to.
