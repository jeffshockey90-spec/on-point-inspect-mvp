# UI Audit — FLOW App

I went through the screens and the code behind them looking for things that would look wrong, confuse someone, or embarrass the business. I found one real data-accuracy bug on the Invoices screen that's worth fixing soon, and one app-wide polish issue (the pop-up boxes the app uses for errors/confirmations) that's worth cleaning up when there's time. I did not find anything broken enough to call "fix now."

## Findings

### Invoice due dates show a day earlier than you actually set them, and invoices can get flagged "overdue" before they really are

- **What's happening:** When you type a due date into an invoice (say, the 10th), the Invoices list page shows it as the 9th instead. The same off-by-one problem also makes the page mark an invoice "OVERDUE" starting the evening *before* the real due date, instead of waiting until the due date has actually passed. This is because the page reads the date you typed and assumes it's midnight in a time zone across the ocean, then re-displays it in your local time — which lands it on the previous day. The good news: your printed/emailed invoices and the Schedule screen do NOT have this problem — someone already fixed it there, just not on this one screen.
- **Why it matters to you:** A client could see (or you could see) an invoice marked "overdue" before it actually is, which looks sloppy or pushy. And the due date on your screen won't match the due date on the invoice you actually send or print — which is confusing if you're ever comparing the two side by side.
- **How urgent:** Fix soon — it's not losing anyone money, but it makes the Invoices screen show wrong information every time a due date is involved.
- **Where it is:** The Invoices list screen (the page that lists all your invoices and their status) — `app/invoices/page.tsx`, in the `formatDate` and `isOverdue` functions.
- **What I'd do:** Have the builder copy the same day-safe date fix that's already used on the Schedule screen and the printed invoice (`app/invoices/[id]/print/page.tsx`) into this file too, so all three agree.

### Error and confirmation messages pop up as plain browser alert boxes instead of the app's own styled message boxes

- **What's happening:** In about 40 places across the app — including sending agreements, saving payment info, and editing signed agreements — when something goes wrong (or succeeds), the app shows a plain gray system pop-up box instead of a message that matches the rest of the app's look.
- **Why it matters to you:** These boxes look like something broke, even when nothing did — they're the same kind of pop-up a browser shows for a security warning, not something a client expects from a polished, paid product. They also look especially out of place inside the iPhone app, where a web-style pop-up stands out from the native-feeling screens around it. It doesn't lose data or money, but it's the kind of thing that chips away at "this looks professional."
- **How urgent:** Worth doing eventually — it's cosmetic, not a functional problem, and nothing is broken.
- **Where it is:** Scattered across many screens — most visibly on the agreement-sending button, the payment/invoice panel, and the signed-agreement editor (`components/SendAgreementButton.tsx`, `components/PaymentInvoicePanel.tsx`, `app/reports/[id]/signed-agreement/[agreementId]/SignedAgreementEditor.tsx`, and ~37 other files).
- **What I'd do:** Swap these plain pop-ups for the app's existing styled banner/toast message style one screen at a time, starting with the ones clients (not just you and your inspectors) can see — agreement signing and payment.

## What I didn't find

I checked the color/theme system (the thing that keeps light and dark mode looking right) and it's clean — no hardcoded colors breaking it. I also checked for obviously broken links, missing images, and layout code that would overflow on a phone screen, and didn't find anything concrete enough to report. I did not spin up the app in a browser to click through it visually — this was a code-only read-through, so purely visual issues (spacing, alignment, things that only show up when you actually look at a rendered screen) wouldn't have been caught.
