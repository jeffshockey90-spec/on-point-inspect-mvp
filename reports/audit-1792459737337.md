# How easy is FLOW for an inspector switching over, and how would they build their first report?

**Short answer:** FLOW is genuinely well set up for a newcomer on the surface — there's a welcome tour, a step-by-step setup checklist, and a "load sample data" button so someone can poke around before risking a real job. The rough part isn't getting *in* the door, it's the first ten minutes *after*: the "create a job" form is one long page of everything at once, and once a job exists, the inspector lands on a report screen packed with a couple dozen tools before they've typed a single finding. There are also two different ways to actually fill out a report (typing into the website, or using the phone-based AI camera tool) and nothing on screen tells a new person which one to use first.

**How I think a new inspector would actually try to build their first report:** They'd click "New Inspection" from the home screen, fill in the client/property/pricing form (which is one continuous page — name, email, address, service type, pricing, mold/radon samples, agreement, all in one scroll), hit save, and get dropped straight onto that job's report page. From there, I'd expect most people to instinctively reach for their phone and open the "Field Tool" to walk the house and let the camera/AI do the writing — that's clearly the flagship, most-polished part of the product. A smaller number, especially anyone used to typing reports on a laptop, would instead try typing findings directly into the sections on the report page itself. Both paths work and both save to the same report — but nothing explains that up front, so it's a guess rather than something the software tells them.

## Findings

### Two ways to fill out a report, and nothing tells you which one to use
- **What's happening:** You can build a report's content two different ways — typing straight into the report page section-by-section on a computer, or using the Field Tool on a phone, which uses photos, voice, and AI to write things for you. Both are fully built and both work, but there's nothing on screen that says "do it this way first" or explains when you'd want one over the other.
- **Why it matters to you:** Someone switching from another inspection program has probably built their whole mental habit around "type into boxes." If they don't realize the phone/AI tool exists (or try it once, bail because it feels different, and go back to typing), they'll miss the feature that actually makes FLOW faster than what they used before — and may conclude FLOW is just "a slower version of my old software."
- **How urgent:** Worth doing eventually — nothing is broken, it's a guidance gap. A sentence of in-app pointing (e.g. on the report page: "Tip: most inspectors fill this in from their phone using the Field Tool while walking the house") would close most of it.
- **Where it is:** The job/report screen you land on after scheduling an inspection (`app/reports/[id]/page.tsx`) versus the mobile camera/AI tool (`app/field/page.tsx`).
- **What I'd do:** Add one short, visible nudge on the report screen pointing new users to the Field Tool as the recommended starting point, before they start typing manually.

### The report screen shows two dozen tools before you've entered anything
- **What's happening:** The moment a new job is created, the report screen that opens shows a long list of AI and admin tools at once — things like "AI Report Review," "House Intelligence," "Live AI Inspector Assistant," "Connected Finding Intelligence," "Activity Timeline," "Publish Guard," repair-request history, and more — all visible before a single finding or photo has been added.
- **Why it matters to you:** For an experienced FLOW user these are time-savers. For someone on day one, with an empty report, it reads as "overwhelming software with a hundred buttons," which is exactly the first impression you don't want a switching inspector to have. It can make the software feel harder to learn than it actually is.
- **How urgent:** Worth doing eventually — this is a first-impression/ease-of-learning issue, not something that's broken.
- **Where it is:** The job/report screen (`app/reports/[id]/page.tsx`, the toolbox pulled in around the "InspectorToolsDrawer" section).
- **What I'd do:** On a brand-new, empty report, show just the "add your first finding" action front and center, and tuck the advanced tools into a "more tools" area until there's actual content to use them on.

### Scheduling a new job is one very long page, not a short series of steps
- **What's happening:** The "New Inspection" screen (client info, co-buyer, realtor, property address, service type, add-on services, pricing, mold/radon sample counts, agreement, discount, notes) is all on one continuously scrolling page rather than broken into a few short steps.
- **Why it matters to you:** It's not confusing exactly, but it's a lot to take in at once for someone's very first job in a new system — long forms feel heavier and more error-prone than short ones, especially on a phone screen while juggling a client on the other end of a call.
- **How urgent:** Worth doing eventually — a polish item, not a blocker.
- **Where it is:** The "New Inspection" screen you use to schedule a job (`app/inspections/new/page.tsx`).
- **What I'd do:** Group the form into a few clearly labeled sections (Client, Property, Pricing, Agreement) with headers you can jump between, even if it stays on one page.

### The home screen's full tool list is 23 items deep, shown as one flat grid
- **What's happening:** Below the welcome tour and the setup checklist, the home screen's "All Tools" section lists 23 different features side by side (Reports, Analytics, Timesheets, Pay Splits, Day Route, Developer API, Realtors, Invoices, Radon, Mold, Equipment Analyzer, Code Assistant, and more) with no grouping by how often a new user would actually need them.
- **Why it matters to you:** The welcome tour and checklist do a good job pointing a brand-new inspector at the handful of things that matter on day one. But the moment they scroll past that onto "All Tools," it's a wall of equally-sized options, which can make the software feel bigger and more intimidating than it needs to for someone still learning the basics.
- **How urgent:** Worth doing eventually — this is softened by the tour and checklist already in place, so it's not urgent.
- **Where it is:** The main home/dashboard screen after logging in (`app/page.tsx`, the "All Tools" grid).
- **What I'd do:** Group that grid under a couple of plain headings like "Day to day" and "Business & admin" so new users' eyes go to the right handful of things first.

### Two separate "getting set up" checklists that don't quite match
- **What's happening:** There are two different setup checklists: one lives on its own page ("Getting Started," reached from the home screen) and tracks 5 milestones; a second, shorter one is embedded directly on the home screen and tracks a different set of steps pulled from a different source.
- **Why it matters to you:** This is a small thing, but a new inspector who dismisses one checklist might assume they're "done" with setup while the other checklist (with different steps) still shows things unfinished — or vice versa. It's a minor consistency wrinkle, not something that blocks anyone.
- **How urgent:** Worth doing eventually — cosmetic/consistency only.
- **Where it is:** The "Getting Started" page (`app/onboarding/page.tsx` and `components/OnboardingChecklist.tsx`) versus the shorter checklist embedded on the home screen (`components/SetupChecklist.tsx`).
- **What I'd do:** Point both checklists at the same underlying list of setup steps so they always agree.

### A job is called an "Inspection" when you create it, then a "Report" everywhere after
- **What's happening:** You schedule something called an "Inspection" on the "New Inspection" screen, but the very next screen you land on — and basically everywhere after that — calls the same job a "Report."
- **Why it matters to you:** It's the same job under two names depending on where you're standing. Most people won't even notice, but someone switching from software that used one consistent word for "the job" might pause for a second wondering if they're looking at two different things.
- **How urgent:** Worth doing eventually — very minor wording consistency, not a functional issue.
- **Where it is:** "New Inspection" screen (`app/inspections/new/page.tsx`) versus the job screen it sends you to (`app/reports/[id]/page.tsx`).
- **What I'd do:** No action needed unless you want tighter wording consistency — low priority.

## What's already working well for a new inspector

- **There's a real welcome tour.** A five-step guided walkthrough points at "New Inspection," the Field Tool, Schedule, the day's numbers, and the Getting Started guide the first time someone lands on the home screen, and it can be replayed any time with a "Take the Tour" button.
- **There's a genuine setup checklist**, not just a tour, walking through company info, agreements, scheduling a first job, filling in a report, and the Field Tool — with a visible progress bar.
- **There's a one-click "sample data" option**, clearly intended to let a brand-new inspector try the whole workflow — schedule, report, AI review, delivery — on a fake job with zero risk before touching a real client's information.
- **Account creation is quick** — one short form, and it drops you straight into the software afterward instead of making you log in again separately.
