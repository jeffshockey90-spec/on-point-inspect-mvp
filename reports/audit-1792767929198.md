# Are signatures really being confirmed, or could it just look signed?

**Short answer: You're good. A report can only be marked "Signed" after the client actually checks the "I agree" box, actually draws or types their name in the signature box, and the system actually saves it. There is no way for someone to look signed without truly doing all of that — and there's no button anywhere (not even for you) that can quietly flip someone to "Signed" without them going through that process.** The only thing that can make an unsigned client show as not needing a signature is the separate "Waive" feature, and that one is clearly labeled as a waiver, not a signature — it never shows up looking like the client actually signed. Details below.

## Findings

### 1. A client can't be marked "Signed" without checking the box, signing, and the save going through
- **What's happening:** Before the client can even press the sign button, they must check a box that says they've read and agree to the agreement and consent to sign electronically, and they must actually draw or type their name in the signature box. If either one is missing, the button won't even work. On top of that, the system itself double-checks both of those same things again before it will save anything as signed — so even someone trying to trick the page can't skip that check.
- **Why it matters to you:** This means "Signed" on your report truly means the client went through the full process — checked the box, put down a signature, and it was saved. You're not at risk of a report showing "Signed" when a client only opened the link and didn't finish.
- **How urgent:** N/A — this is working correctly, confirmed by reading the actual code.
- **Where it is:** The sign-agreement screen clients open from their link (`app/client-agreement/[id]/AgreementSignatureForm.tsx`), double-checked by the saving process behind the scenes (`app/api/sign-agreement/route.ts`).
- **What I'd do:** Nothing — no changes needed here.

### 2. There is no "mark as signed" shortcut anywhere — only a clearly separate "Waive" option
- **What's happening:** I looked everywhere a report's signed status could possibly get flipped to "Signed" — your reports screen, the client contacts screen, the agreements list, even the sample/demo data setup — and the only place that ever sets someone to "Signed" is the real signing process itself, triggered by the client's own action. The one thing you (or your team) CAN do manually is "Waive" the signature requirement for a client (for example, if you already have a paper copy on file). That's a totally separate action with its own purple box, its own required reason, and its own "Waived by [you], on [date]" note — the app never lets a waiver look like the client actually signed.
- **Why it matters to you:** You can trust that "Signed" always means the client did it themselves. If a client denies ever signing something, you can also tell from looking at a report whether that job used the real signature path or a waiver, because they look and read completely differently.
- **How urgent:** N/A — already correct, confirmed by reading the actual code.
- **Where it is:** Agreement area inside a report (`components/AgreementStatusPanel.tsx`), the actual signing save process (`app/api/sign-agreement/route.ts`).
- **What I'd do:** Nothing — no changes needed here.

### 3. If a client's phone or internet drops right as they sign, the page may show an error even though the signature actually saved
- **What's happening:** If a client signs on a weak connection (common out in the field or in a house with bad signal) and the connection drops right as they hit "Sign," there's a chance the signature was saved on the server's side, but the confirmation message never made it back to their phone — so they might see an error message telling them to try again. The system is built to handle this safely: if they tap "Sign" again, it recognizes the agreement is already signed and just confirms it rather than creating a duplicate or an error.
- **Why it matters to you:** The worst-case outcome here is a client being mildly annoyed by an error message on a signature that actually went through fine — not a case of a signature being lost or faked. If a client ever tells you "it said it failed, but did it actually work?", the answer is very likely yes — you can just check the report, or have them refresh and try tapping sign once more to confirm it on their end too.
- **How urgent:** Worth doing eventually — this is a minor annoyance, not a real risk to your signatures.
- **Where it is:** The sign-agreement screen clients use (`app/client-agreement/[id]/AgreementSignatureForm.tsx`).
- **What I'd do:** Nothing urgent — just good to know if a client ever reports a confusing "it failed" message after signing.
