# How easy is FLOW for a new inspector to pick up?

Overall it's in decent shape for day-to-day field work — there's a real "getting started" checklist, a sample-data demo mode, and a built-in assistant for business questions. But the welcome screen a brand-new inspector actually sees is aimed at a business owner setting up a company, not at someone just joining an existing team, and one error message in Settings would show a raw code word instead of plain English if an inspector ever hit it. Nothing here is dangerous to clients or money — these are "new person gets confused on day one" problems, not safety or billing problems.

## Findings

### A brand-new inspector's very first screen tells them to do things that aren't theirs to do
- **What's happening:** When someone you've just invited sets their password and logs in for the first time, they have zero inspections assigned to them yet. The app automatically sends anyone with zero inspections to a "Set up FLOW" welcome page. That page's first two to-do items are "add your company logo, contact info, and starting address" and "set up your agreement paperwork and Standards of Practice" — both of which are company-wide settings that only the account owner is allowed to save, and that you've presumably already set up for the business.
- **Why it matters to you:** A new hire's first impression of FLOW is a checklist telling them the company "isn't set up yet" and pointing them at settings they don't actually have permission to change. Best case, it's confusing and makes the software look unfinished. It also means they won't be shown anything specific to them — like "here's how to start your first inspection" or "here's how the camera tool works."
- **How urgent:** Worth doing eventually — it's a first-impression issue, not something that blocks work, since the real "create an inspection" and "open field tool" steps are also on that same page.
- **Where it is:** The "Set up FLOW" welcome page new users land on (`app/onboarding/page.tsx` and `components/OnboardingChecklist.tsx`), triggered automatically from the main dashboard (`app/page.tsx`) any time someone has no inspections yet.
- **What I'd do:** Show a different, shorter welcome page for someone who's joining a company that's already set up — just "here's how to start your first inspection" and "here's the field tool" — and only show the full company setup checklist to the actual owner.

### If an inspector ever tries to save something in Settings they're not allowed to touch, they'd see a confusing error
- **What's happening:** Regular inspectors (not owners) can open the Settings page and see the full company setup form - logo, pricing, paperwork wording, and so on - same as you do. If one of them filled it out and hit save, the system correctly blocks the save (good - that protection is solid), but the message that comes back to them would literally read the word "owner_only" rather than something like "Only the account owner can change company settings."
- **Why it matters to you:** It's not a safety or money issue since the save is blocked either way. But if an inspector ever does try to update something in Settings, they'd be stuck looking at an unexplained code word with no idea what it means or who to ask.
- **How urgent:** Fix soon - it's a quick wording fix and avoids a confusing dead end if anyone runs into it.
- **Where it is:** The Settings page (`app/settings/page.tsx`, around the save-handler's owner check).
- **What I'd do:** Translate that message to plain English, like "Only the account owner can save company settings - ask them to make this change."

### There's no in-app "how do I..." help - just a chat straight to you
- **What's happening:** The built-in "Ask FLOW" assistant is genuinely useful, but it only answers questions about your business data (like "how many roof issues did I write up this month" or "what's still unpaid") - it can't answer "how do I attach a photo" or "where do I set my price." The only other help option in the app is a support chat that goes directly to you personally.
- **Why it matters to you:** For a new inspector, every "how do I..." question - however small - turns into a message to you, instead of something they can look up themselves. That's extra interruptions for you, especially in the first few weeks of someone joining.
- **How urgent:** Worth doing eventually - not broken, just a gap. The "getting started" checklist and the four-step "test drive with sample data" walkthrough already on that welcome page do a reasonable job covering the basics.
- **Where it is:** The "Ask FLOW" assistant (`app/ask-flow`, `lib/ai/askFlow.ts`) and the Support page (`app/support/page.tsx`).
- **What I'd do:** Either let Ask FLOW also answer "how do I use this screen" questions, or add a short written guide/FAQ for the basics (starting an inspection, using the field tool, sending a report) so new inspectors have somewhere to look before messaging you.

## What's working well
- Inviting a new inspector is simple on your end - name, email, send - and they get a clean "set your password" email and land in the app automatically assigned to your company and team.
- The welcome page includes a real four-step practice run using sample data (create a sample inspection, review it, run the AI check, try sending it) before anyone touches a real client's job - that's a solid, low-risk way to learn the tool.
- The "What's New" page and suggestion box give inspectors a way to see recent changes and flag problems or ideas, with visible replies from you - good for keeping a growing team in the loop without extra meetings.
