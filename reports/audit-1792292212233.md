# FLOW health check — October 18, 2026

**Overall:** The app is in solid shape. I went through the areas most likely to cause real trouble — money-handling, client-facing pages that don't require a login, and a few known trouble spots — and didn't find anything on fire. There are 3 things worth your attention below, none of them urgent emergencies, but one of them is worth fixing soon because it quietly hides a record you might want later.

---

### A payment receipt gets emailed, but doesn't get filed away

- **What's happening:** When a client pays online through Stripe, the system does correctly email them a receipt. But right after sending it, the step that's supposed to file a copy of "this receipt was sent" into your records fails every time, silently, because of a small mismatch in how it's saved.
- **Why it matters to you:** The client still gets their receipt — nothing is wrong on their end. But if you ever go looking at an inspection's "Sent" history to double-check that a payment receipt went out (for example, if a client claims they never got one, or you're double-checking your own records), you won't find it listed. It looks like nothing was ever sent, even though it was.
- **How urgent:** Fix soon — it doesn't affect anyone's money or break anything visible, but it quietly erases a record you might need.
- **Where it is:** The behind-the-scenes step that runs after a client completes an online payment (`app/api/stripe-webhook/route.ts`).
- **What I'd do:** Have the builder correct the one line that's saving the record incorrectly so the receipt shows up in the "Sent" history like every other email does.

### The online-payment setup is duplicated in two places

- **What's happening:** The logic that checks "is this inspector properly set up to accept card payments" and calculates the card-processing fee exists in one shared, central spot for inspection payments — and that's intentional, so everyone pays the same way. But the separate flow for paying a standalone invoice copied that same logic into its own file instead of reusing the shared one.
- **Why it matters to you:** Right now both copies behave the same, so nothing is broken today. The risk is down the road: if you ever change how fees are calculated or how Stripe accounts get approved, it's easy to update the shared spot and forget the second copy exists — and then invoice payments and inspection payments could quietly start behaving differently from each other.
- **How urgent:** Worth doing eventually — nothing is wrong right now, this is about preventing a future mismatch.
- **Where it is:** The "pay this invoice" link clients receive (`app/api/invoices/[id]/checkout/route.ts`), versus the main shared payment setup (`lib/stripeCheckout.ts`).
- **What I'd do:** Have the builder point the invoice payment flow at the same shared file the rest of the app uses, instead of keeping its own copy.

### A few of the report-editing screens are built from very large files

- **What's happening:** The code behind your biggest, most-used screens — the report editor and the client's shareable report page — are each roughly 5,000 lines long, all in one file.
- **Why it matters to you:** It's not broken today. But files that size are harder for a builder (human or AI) to safely change without accidentally affecting something unrelated elsewhere on the same screen. Over time this slightly raises the odds that a future fix introduces an unexpected side effect somewhere else on that page.
- **How urgent:** Worth doing eventually — a cleanliness/maintainability note, not an active problem.
- **Where it is:** The report editor (`app/reports/[id]/page.tsx` and `app/reports/[id]/ReportFindingsSortable.tsx`) and the client-facing shared report (`app/share/[id]/page.tsx`).
- **What I'd do:** No rush — if a builder is already working in one of these files for an unrelated fix, it'd be a good time to split it into smaller pieces, but it doesn't need its own dedicated project.
